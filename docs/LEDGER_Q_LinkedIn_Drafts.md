## Day 3 — The conflicting assay must remain visible

**Draft**

94.2% versus 99.1% is not a styling detail.

In LEDGER-Q’s synthetic Drug Q case, the original assay and a transcription disagree for the same batch and method. The approved repeat-test rationale is absent.

The result is HOLD. The original stays visible. Root cause remains UNKNOWN, and batch disposition is not decided.

I wanted the work sample to demonstrate a documentation habit: preserve the inconvenient evidence instead of selecting the number that makes the record look better.

The software can organize this conflict. A qualified professional remains responsible for investigating and deciding what the evidence means.

## Day 4 — A corrective action is not proof of closure

**Draft**

Writing an action does not prove it worked.

My LEDGER-Q portfolio case keeps three questions visible: has a human established the cause, is implementation supported by evidence, and is effectiveness supported by evidence?

The synthetic CAPA record cannot close when those critical supports are missing. A due date or completed-looking screen does not replace them.

Building this workflow made the distinction between action tracking and evidence-supported closure clearer to me.

It is a work sample of controlled documentation support. I am not claiming that this fictional investigation establishes a real root cause or a real batch decision.

#CAPA #PharmaceuticalQuality #QualitySystems

**Suggested media:** Three-column visual: cause evidence / implementation evidence / effectiveness evidence, with the missing fields highlighted. Use the synthetic case and avoid compliance badges.

## Day 5 — A changed source changes the review question

**Draft**

An old approval should not silently approve new evidence.

LEDGER-Q keeps saved links between sources, quality events, corrective actions, training and AI use. When a source changes, affected work reopens for fresh review while the previous history remains inspectable.

That design is more useful than treating every record as an isolated file.

The synthetic checks exercise linked invalidation and historical reconstruction. They do not prove every customer process or a globally scaled enterprise deployment.

For real use, the organization still needs named review responsibilities and its own acceptance criteria.

#ChangeControl #DataIntegrity #QualityAssurance

**Suggested media:** Dependency graph with one changed source and the affected nodes marked. Distinguish an explanatory diagram from a screenshot of saved application records.

## Day 6 — AI support, with an accountable boundary

**Draft**

The interesting question is not how many agents are on a diagram. It is what their output is allowed to do.

## Day 8 — Tests with clear scopes

**Draft**

A passing test count is useful only when its scope is clear.

For LEDGER-Q, I keep separate evidence groups: 55 local synthetic API/storage integration checks, 9 server-rendered presentation checks, and 6 hosted synthetic fresh-entry/proof-separation checks recorded for the relevant release.

They cover different questions. They are not an accuracy percentage, clinical proof, regulatory validation or complete browser testing.

The important adverse cases include missing evidence, wrong roles, changed revisions, unsupported candidates and recovery boundaries.

Professional quality still needs qualified expected answers, unseen authorized cases and a supervised pilot.
